# Data processing in the Translog Quotation Desk

*A factual record of what this system does with data, written from the code
on 2026-10-02. It is an engineering inventory, not a privacy policy and not a
statement of legal compliance. Items marked **REQUIRES INPUT** need a decision
or a fact from Translog Express; the whole document should be reviewed by a
qualified privacy/legal professional before it is relied on.*

## What the system is

An internal operations tool. Authorised Translog Express operators sign in to
a web dashboard that reads quotation enquiries arriving by email, extracts the
shipment details with a language model, drafts clarification questions (sent
only after an operator approves them), searches live air-freight rates, and
presents a quotation for an operator to approve before it is emailed to the
client. There is no public website, no customer-facing account, no signup, no
payment, no analytics and no advertising.

## Who the data is about

- **Clients of Translog Express** who email a quotation enquiry: the sender's
  name and email address, the subject and body of their emails (which contain
  shipment details and may contain anything else the client wrote), and any
  reply they send.
- **Operators**: the name an operator types when approving a clarification,
  a quotation or a goods type, or when resolving a manual-review request.
  Operators share one dashboard access key; there are no per-operator
  accounts.

## What is collected and why

| Data | Source | Why it is processed |
|---|---|---|
| Client email: from address, subject, body, message ids, received time | Gmail mailbox(es) the business connects (read-only token) | To recognise an enquiry, extract the shipment, correlate replies to the right request |
| Extracted shipment record (origin, destination, weight, dimensions, commodity, cargo type, chemical status, MSDS status, pieces, delivery type, delivery address, ship date) | Produced by the extraction model from the email text | To validate the enquiry and run a rate search |
| Clarification and quotation emails sent to the client | Composed by the system, released by an operator | To obtain missing details and to deliver the quotation |
| Rate-search results (carrier, price, transit) | WebCargo, via the browser worker | To select and quote a rate |
| Operator name on each approval/decision | Typed by the operator | Accountability: the audit trail records who released each email |
| Audit trail events (request id, event type, timestamp, field names, counts, states, decisions) | Generated by the workflow | Evidence that every send had a human behind it; operations troubleshooting |
| Session cookie `translog_session` (HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Strict, 12 h) | Set by the server on sign-in | Keeping an operator signed in. Strictly necessary; it is the only cookie the dashboard sets. No analytics or tracking cookies exist. |

The audit trail deliberately records **field names, counts and states, never
email bodies or addresses**.

## Where it is stored

- **Persistent request state** (shipment records, request state, operator
  names on decisions, audit log): on the hosting platform's persistent disk
  (Render, mount `/var/lib/translog`), or in the configured Redis when the
  Redis backend is selected.
- **Rate-search jobs and results**: in the shared managed Redis (Upstash)
  that joins the dashboard to the browser worker, with configured TTLs
  (results 24 h, failures 7 days by default).
- **The WebCargo browser session**: in a persistent Chromium profile on the
  worker machine.
- **Email**: remains in the Gmail mailbox; the system reads it and sends
  through it. Nothing is downloaded to a second mail store.
- **Logs**: the platform's log stream. Logs name request ids, message ids,
  event types and error classes; they are written not to contain email
  bodies, addresses or credentials.
- **Retention**: **REQUIRES INPUT** — no deletion schedule is implemented.
  Settled requests leave the dashboard view after one hour but stay in the
  store and the audit trail indefinitely.

## Third parties that receive data

| Recipient | What they receive | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Google (Gmail API) | Everything in the connected mailbox is read through it; outbound emails are sent through it | Mail access |
| OpenRouter → the configured model (Qwen) | The text of each client email | Shipment extraction |
| WebCargo (Freightos) | Origin and destination airports, weight, dimensions, pieces, goods type, ship date — never the client's name or address | Live rate search |
| Upstash (managed Redis) | Rate-search job requests and results, worker status | Queue between dashboard and worker |
| Render | Hosts the dashboard process and its persistent disk | Hosting |

**REQUIRES INPUT**: data-processing agreements or terms with each recipient,
and the lawful basis under which client correspondence is sent to the
extraction model, are a business/legal matter not established in this
repository.

## Who can see it

Anyone holding the dashboard access key. The key is a single shared secret;
the system cannot tell operators apart beyond the name they type. Access is
limited by the Host allowlist, the sign-in page, a 12-hour signed session,
and sign-in throttling. **REQUIRES INPUT**: who holds the key, how it is
rotated, and whether per-operator accounts are required.

## Jurisdiction

The business and its clients appear to be in India and the Gulf; the
extraction model and hosting are operated by companies in other countries.
Whether India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, the GDPR for any
European client, or other regimes apply — and what they require of the
transfers above — **requires review by a qualified professional**. Nothing in
this document claims compliance with any of them.
